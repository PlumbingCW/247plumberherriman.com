Emergency Drain Cleaning in Herriman, UT.

Water that pools around your ankles in the shower, a kitchen sink that gurgles and refuses to drain, or a floor drain that burps up gray water all point to the same thing. Something is restricting the pipe, and Herriman drain cleaning starts by working out where.

Start with how many drains are slow

The number of affected fixtures tells you most of what you need to know. One slow sink means a clog in that fixture's trap or branch line, usually within 15 feet of the drain. Two fixtures on the same floor point to a shared branch. Every drain in the house, with toilets bubbling, points to the main drain or the sewer lateral.

Plumbers also read the pattern of the symptom. A drain that empties slowly and gets worse over weeks is a partial restriction, often grease or hair. A drain that works fine and then stops dead overnight is more often a foreign object or a wipe that lodged in a bend.

Gurgling is the other clue. When a tub drains and the toilet burps, air is being pulled past a blockage downstream. That sound is a reason to stop using water, not to run one more load of laundry.

Backed up right now? Do this while you wait

A drain that is overflowing into a bathroom or a basement is an emergency, and our dispatch line runs 24/7 for exactly that. While a plumber is on the way, the goal is to stop adding water to a pipe that cannot carry it.

  1. Stop all water use.

    No flushing, no dishwasher, no washing machine. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and right now that place is your floor.

  2. Keep electrics dry.

    If water reaches outlets, a furnace, or a basement panel, stay out and leave the breaker alone. Wet floors and power do not mix.

  3. Find the cleanout.

    If you know where the sewer cleanout cap sits, clear a path to it. A plumber can often get a cable in there faster than through a fixture.

  4. Skip the chemical bottle.

    Caustic drain openers sitting in a stopped pipe burn skin and hands when the plumber opens the trap. Tell dispatch if you already poured one in.

If sewage is coming up from a floor drain or toilet and stops when you stop using water, the blockage is probably in your own plumbing. See the section on who owns what, further down this page.

The kitchen sink clog, from the trap to the stack

Kitchen sinks clog differently from every other drain in the house. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe and coats the wall, and food scraps from the disposal stick to it. Over months that coating narrows a 1.5 inch drain to the width of a pencil.

We begin at the P-trap under the sink. Pulling it with a bucket underneath takes a few minutes and tells us whether the clog is local. If the trap is clean, a 25 foot drum auger goes in through the wall stub-out and works toward the vertical stack.

A double-bowl sink with a disposal adds a second trap and a baffle fitting to check. When the dishwasher drain hose ties into the disposal, a clog there also sends dirty water back into the dishwasher tub, which is a useful symptom to mention on the call. If the disposal itself is jammed or leaking, the garbage disposal repair page covers that side.

Drain snaking, explained without the jargon

Snaking is the everyday name for cabling a drain. It is the most common method and the right first move for most clogs.

Hand auger.

A hand-cranked coil with a corkscrew tip, 6 to 25 feet long. It suits sinks, tubs and shower drains where the clog sits close to the opening.

Drum machine.

A motor drives a spring cable stored in a drum, 1/4 to 5/8 inch thick. Interchangeable heads cut, scrape or retrieve depending on the blockage.

Sectional cable.

Used on main drains, this rig threads 100 feet or more of heavier cable through a cleanout. A cutter head grinds through roots and soft debris.

A cable clears a path, but it does not scrub the entire pipe wall. That limit is why a hydro jet is sometimes the better follow-up, as covered on the hydro jetting page.

Shower and tub drains that will not clear

Shower and tub clogs are mostly hair wrapped around soap scum. Herriman's water is very hard according to the city, with wells in the 25 to 60 grains per gallon range and JVWCD water around 10 to 12. Hard water leaves soap curd, a sticky mineral film that grabs hair and builds faster than it does with soft water.

A tub drain has a stopper mechanism, a pop-up linkage or a trip lever, that often catches most of the hair. We pull it, clean it, and run a short cable through the overflow tee. A drain screen costs a few dollars and prevents the next call.

If a shower drains slowly in the same bathroom where the tub is fine, the clog sits between the shower trap and the branch. That is a short cable run in most homes.

Where to look for your sewer cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe, usually 3 or 4 inches across, that gives a plumber direct access to the drain without going through a fixture. JBID's lateral rules call for one within 5 feet of the building and another every 100 feet, so newer homes should have at least one. Common spots to check include these.

  • A white or black cap at ground level along the foundation, on the side where the main drain leaves the house.
  • A capped Y-fitting in an unfinished basement or garage floor near the water heater or laundry area.
  • A green or black lid in a landscaped bed or by the driveway edge, often a few feet from the wall.
  • A cap on the main stack in a crawlspace, accessible only by removing a panel.

If you cannot find a cleanout, say so when you call. Older homes sometimes have none, and adding one is a small job that makes every future cleaning faster and cheaper.

What not to flush, and what not to pour

JBID, the sewer district for Herriman, says only human waste and toilet paper belong in the toilet. Baby wipes, flushable wipes, feminine products, rags and cotton swabs cause backups, even when the package says otherwise.

The same district says never to pour oil or grease down sinks, toilets or garbage disposals. Let it cool, solidify it in a jar or can, and put it in the trash. A sink strainer basket catches the rest.

A disposal does not make grease safe. It only grinds fat into smaller bits that coat the pipe wall faster.

Who owns the clog, you or the sewer district

Sewer service in Herriman comes from Jordan Basin Improvement District, not the city. The district's rule is simple. If the blockage sits in the private line from your house to the district main, the owner must fix it, and the district cannot legally work on private property.

A quick test comes from JBID itself. If turning off all water use stops the backup, the problem is your house plumbing or your lateral. If the backup continues with nothing running, call JBID at 801-571-1166 or 801-243-8492 after hours, because the main may be involved.

Lateral problems are common in neighborhoods built quickly since the city's 1999 incorporation, including Rosecrest, Anthem and the townhome areas near Herriman Towne Center. A camera pass tells us which side of the property line the trouble sits on. If it does turn out to be a failing lateral, see sewer line repair.

What drain cleaning costs and why it varies

We give a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that move the number.

  • Access pointA clog reached through a sink trap costs less than one that needs a cable fed in from a cleanout or a roof vent.
  • Distance and depthA clog 8 feet from the drain is a quick job. One 70 feet out in the lateral takes a larger machine and more time.
  • Cause of the clogHair and soap clear fast. Roots or a collapsed section need cutting tools, a camera, and sometimes a repair plan.
  • Camera inspectionAdding a camera pass after cleaning costs extra but shows the pipe condition and gives you a recording.
  • After-hours timingNight, weekend and holiday visits usually carry a different rate than a same-day appointment when available.

A sudden, covered backup may be paid by an insurance policy, but sewer backup coverage is often an add-on and wear-related clogs are usually excluded. JBID also reminds owners that backups are not automatically covered, so check your policy before you need it.

When cleaning is not enough

Roots enter at joints and cracks, then grow into dense mats that catch every wipe and strand of hair. A cable can cut them back, but they return within a year or two unless the joint is repaired. If the camera shows roots, the next conversation is about the lateral, not the sink.

A drain line that leaks is less obvious. A damp spot on a basement wall or a slab that stays wet after the drain runs suggests a cracked branch. We find those with a camera and a leak locator. The leak detection page explains that process.

Drain cleaning questions from Herriman homeowners

Do you handle drain cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Herriman?

Yes, dispatch runs around the clock and we send a licensed plumber when one is available. We do not promise an arrival time, because it depends on the schedule and your address. Call (385) 488-1892, describe the backup, and we will tell you what to do while you wait.

How do I know if the clog is in my sink or in the main line?

Run water in a different fixture. If only one drain is slow, the clog is local. If multiple drains slow down, or a toilet gurgles when you drain the tub, the blockage is further down. A basement floor drain that backs up is almost always on the main.

Is snaking a drain different from hydro jetting?

Yes. A cable punches or cuts through a blockage and leaves a hole the size of its head. A jet scours the whole pipe wall with high-pressure water. Cables are cheaper and faster for typical clogs. Jetting suits grease and root mats, which the hydro jetting page explains.

Can I use a plunger or drain cleaner first?

A plunger is fine for a toilet or a single fixture. Chemical drain openers are a poor idea, because they can sit in a stopped pipe and burn the plumber who opens the trap. They also do little against hair, wipes or roots.

Why does my kitchen sink clog again every few months?

Usually because grease coated the pipe wall and each new batch of scraps sticks to it. A cable opens a channel, but the coating remains. Hot water does not fix it. Changing what goes down the drain and scheduling a jetting pass are more effective.

Who is responsible if the sewer line is blocked?

The private line from your house to the district main belongs to you. JBID handles its own mains. The district's own guidance is that if a backup stops when all water use stops, the problem is in your plumbing. See sewer line cleaning for main line work.

Do you offer a camera inspection with drain cleaning?

Yes, on request. A push camera on a flexible rod shows the pipe interior and records the run. It is most useful after repeat clogs, before buying a house, or when a cable brings back clay or roots.

Will drain cleaning damage my pipes?

Not when it is done correctly. A properly sized cable and head cleans without scraping through healthy pipe. Older cast iron or cracked clay can be fragile, and a plumber will pick a gentler method if the camera shows it.
