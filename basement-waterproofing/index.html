Basement Waterproofing in Herriman, UT.

A white crust on the block, a dark line where the floor meets the wall or a musty smell after the first big storm means water is finding your basement. Basement waterproofing in Herriman starts with learning where that water comes from before anyone cuts concrete.

We handle the plumbing side, interior drain channels, sump pits and discharge lines. We also say plainly what we do not do, which is structural foundation repair and exterior excavation unless dispatch confirms a specific job.

Read the symptom before choosing a system

Dampness on a basement wall can be condensation, a leaking window well, a cracked wall or groundwater pushing through the slab joint. The fix for each is different.

A simple test is to tape a square of plastic sheeting to the wall for two days. Moisture under the plastic comes from outside, while droplets on the surface come from room air.

Interior drain, exterior membrane or grading fix

Most homes do best with a mix. Fix the surface first, then add interior control if water persists.

Five checks before you spend money

Walk the outside of the house first.

  • Downspouts empty several feet from the foundation, not at the wall..
  • Soil near the house slopes away and has not settled into a trough..
  • Window wells are clear of leaves and have a working drain..
  • Sprinklers do not spray against the foundation..
  • Hose bibs and the clothes washer hoses are not leaking inside the wall..

If all five pass and water still shows up, it is time to look at drainage under the slab.

What the city says about grading

Herriman requires every property to manage drainage on its own lot or send it to the street or a recorded easement. The city cites IRC R401.3, which calls for the grade to fall at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet away from the foundation, or a 5 percent slope where something blocks that distance.

Hard surfaces such as patios and walkways within 10 feet need at least a 2 percent slope away from the house. Measure yours with a level and a tape. A bubble that says the patio tilts toward the wall explains a lot of wet basements.

How an interior system gets installed

A typical job follows this order.

  1. Mark and cut.

    A concrete saw opens a strip along the wall, a foot or so wide.

  2. Dig and lay the channel.

    A perforated drain pipe sits in washed gravel, sloped toward the sump.

  3. Set the sump basin.

    A sealed pit collects the flow, and a pump moves it out. See our sump pump installation page.

  4. Run the discharge.

    The line leaves the home to a spot approved by the building department.

  5. Patch and test.

    New concrete goes over the channel, and we fill the system with water to confirm it flows.

Adding new plumbing lines can require a city permit. Confirm the route before digging.

Herriman soils, water and your slab

The Utah Geological Survey mapped shallow groundwater, collapsible soils and expansive soils in the Copperton quadrangle, which covers north Herriman and neighboring towns. It does not say the same for the whole city, so a house on a hill may behave very differently from one in a flat area.

Expansive soils swell when wet and can crack foundations, while collapsible soils settle when wetted. Both reasons favor keeping irrigation off the foundation edge.

Herriman has required a geotechnical report on every footing excavation since October 2006 because soils vary around the city. If you have the report for your lot, it is the best document to show a contractor.

Radon is a separate concern. The geologic survey notes it enters through small foundation cracks and utility pipes, so a sealed sump lid is part of a good job. Test with a kit, since one in three Utah homes has high radon readings according to the state.

What we do not do

We do not do structural foundation repair, wall bracing, helical piers or exterior excavation unless dispatch confirms a specific job. If your wall bows inward or a crack is wider than a pencil, call a structural engineer first, then come back to us for the drainage.

Questions before you waterproof

Can you waterproof my basement from the outside?

Not by default. Exterior excavation and membrane work is a foundation contractor's job, and we only take it if dispatch confirms a specific job.

Do you fix foundation cracks?

We do not repair structural cracks. We handle the drainage and sump work, and we can refer you to the right trade.

Does every Herriman basement need a sump pump?

No. Many stay dry with good grading and gutters. A sump makes sense when water appears at the slab edge after storms.

What is the grading rule for the area around a house?

The city cites IRC R401.3, with a fall of at least 6 inches in the first 10 feet from the foundation. Check with the building department for your lot.

Will an interior drain stop dampness in the wall?

It controls water at the floor but does not seal the wall. Mild wall dampness can remain.

Do I need a permit for a drain and sump?

Adding new plumbing lines can require one, while repair of existing systems does not. Ask the Herriman building department for your job.

How do I know if the water is groundwater or a plumbing leak?

Shut off the main water valve and watch the area for a day. If the dampness continues, groundwater is likely.
