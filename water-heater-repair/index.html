Emergency Water Heater Repair in Herriman, UT.

Cold showers, a tank that rumbles like popcorn, or a puddle by the drain valve all point to something specific inside the heater. Water heater repair in Herriman starts with finding which part failed and whether the tank behind it is still sound.

We test before we replace. You get a clear price before work starts, and a straight answer if the better move is a new heater.

Start with the symptom, not the part

A no-heat call can come from a dozen causes. Guessing wastes money. We test in order, beginning with the supply, then the fuel or power, then the controls, and only then the parts that wear out.

The tools are simple. A multimeter checks element resistance and voltage. A manometer or leak solution checks the gas side. An infrared thermometer shows whether the tank is heating at all, and a pressure gauge on a hose bib shows whether the home runs high water pressure that stresses the tank.

Leaking or smelling gas? Handle that before anything else

A heater problem becomes urgent when water spreads or gas is involved. Our 24/7 line is open, and there are steps worth doing before anyone arrives.

If you smell rotten eggs, do not hunt for the source. Leave the home first.

  1. Gas smell means leave.

    Get everyone and the pets outside, then call 911 and Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689. Do not use switches, garage door openers or a phone indoors.

  2. Water leaking from the tank.

    Close the cold supply valve above the heater and shut off the gas dial or the electric breaker. Water in the pan with a wet floor means the tank may be breached.

  3. Relief valve discharging steam or hot water.

    Keep people away from the discharge tube. That means pressure or temperature is too high. Shut off the fuel and leave the valve alone.

  4. No leak, just no hot water.

    Leave it off if you are unsure and call. A tank sitting cold costs nothing, but repeated relighting of a failing pilot is worth a professional look.

If the relief valve is blowing hot water repeatedly, do not cap it or plug the tube. That valve is the last safety device on the tank.

The anode rod, the cheapest tank insurance

Inside every steel tank hangs a magnesium or aluminum anode rod. It corrodes in place of the tank wall, so when the rod is gone the steel starts to go next. In very hard water the rod is consumed faster because the water carries more dissolved minerals.

We pull the rod with an impact wrench and a 1 1/16 inch socket and measure how much metal is left. A rod down to a bare steel core should be replaced. If the rod is stuck solid or the tank is already leaking at the seam, we tell you replacement is the honest answer.

Herriman scale, sediment and what they sound like

The city reports its wells between 25 and 60 grains per gallon and supplier water between 10 and 12, and it calls the supply very hard. Dissolved calcium and magnesium drop out as the water heats, and the crust settles on the bottom of the tank. Burners then heat the scale, not the water.

Rumbling, popping and slow recovery are the signs. A flush with a hose and a full-port drain valve clears loose sediment. Heavy scale that has hardened into a stone layer may not drain at all, which tells us the tank is near the end.

The city also switches to a winter source from mid-October and returns to wells in spring, so a flush before winter and another in spring suits this area. Test your tap with a kit to see where you sit.

Relief valve, thermal expansion and the expansion tank

The temperature and pressure relief valve opens when pressure or temperature climbs too high. A valve that drips only when the heater fires usually points to thermal expansion. Heated water takes up more room, and in a closed system with a check valve or pressure reducer it has nowhere to go.

The fix is a small expansion tank on the cold line, set to match the home's static pressure with a tire gauge. A water-logged expansion tank, one that has lost its air charge, causes the same drip. We test it by tapping and by checking the air valve.

Replacing the relief valve alone often fails to stop the drip. We find the pressure source first.

What a service visit includes

A water heater service call is built around testing and a clear conversation, not a script.

  1. Safety check.

    We confirm there is no gas leak, the relief valve and discharge tube are intact, and there is no scorching on the jacket.

  2. Test the heat source.

    Voltage and resistance on elements, flame quality and sensor reading on gas units.

  3. Inspect the tank.

    Pan, base, fittings, valve condition, anode access and any rust trails.

  4. Explain options.

    A repair price, a flush price if relevant, and a replacement price if the tank is failing. You choose before we begin.

  5. Complete and confirm.

    We restore power or gas, relight, check for leaks and run hot water to confirm recovery.

What we will not do

We do not seal a leaking tank shell. If the bottom has rusted through, replacing the heater is the only fix that holds. We also will not bypass a safety device, such as jumpering a high-limit switch, and we will not relight a heater that smells of gas until the cause is found.

If your heater is in a shared utility room of a multi-unit building, the building owner may need to approve the work. We can still diagnose the unit and explain what is needed.

What changes the price of a heater repair

Repair prices depend on what failed and how hard it is to reach. Here is what we weigh.

  • The failed part. A thermocouple or element is a small parts cost. A gas control valve or a full burner assembly is a larger one.
  • Access to the unit. A tank in an open garage is quick. One in a tight closet or behind a furnace takes longer.
  • Tank condition. Scale, rust or a stuck drain valve can turn a simple repair into a flush job or a replacement discussion.
  • Safety fixes found on the way. Missing discharge tube, no drain pan or a failed expansion tank may be added to the quote, and you decide.
  • Timing. After-hours calls and weekend visits cost more than a scheduled weekday appointment.

Sudden damage from a failed tank is often covered by homeowner insurance for the water, while the repair of a worn heater and slow leaks usually are not. Ask your insurer which category your claim falls into before you pay out of pocket.

Water heater repair questions

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Herriman?

Yes. The dispatch line runs around the clock, so a leaking tank at 2 a.m. or a dead heater on a holiday weekend still gets answered. The arrival time depends on the day's workload, and we will be honest about it on the call. In the meantime, shut off the cold supply and the fuel.

Why is my hot water running out so fast?

A broken dip tube, a dead lower element, or sediment filling the tank can all shrink the usable hot water. A tank full of scale holds less water than its label says. We test the elements first on electric units and look at the tank floor on gas.

Is it safe to relight the pilot myself?

On a standard gas tank with a clear pilot instruction label, many homeowners do. If the pilot will not stay lit, if you smell gas, or if the burner flame is yellow and sooty, stop and call. Those are signs of a failing part or a venting problem.

Why does my relief valve keep dripping?

Usually pressure from thermal expansion, a failed expansion tank or high incoming water pressure. Occasionally the valve is simply worn out. Replacing the valve alone will not help if the cause is pressure.

Does flushing really help in Herriman?

Very hard water leaves heavy mineral deposits, so flushing helps when the tank is otherwise healthy. If the sediment has hardened or the drain valve is clogged solid, a flush may not clear it. We check before promising anything.

When should I give up on repairing and replace the heater?

Replace when the tank is leaking from the shell, when it is past 12 years with a major part failing, or when repair costs near half a new unit. Hard-water scale and a spent anode shorten the timeline.
