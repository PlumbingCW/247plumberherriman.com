What happens when you call

A person answers the line, not a recording. You describe the problem in plain words, such as water coming through a ceiling, a toilet that will not stop running, or sewage in the floor drain. The dispatcher asks for the address, a callback number and whether the water main is off.

Then the call splits by urgency. Active flooding, sewage backing up and a failed water heater get priority booking. A dripping faucet or a slow tub drain gets the next sensible slot. Either way you hear the price method before anyone starts work, and nothing begins until you agree to it.

While you wait for the plumber

These steps limit damage in the first few minutes. None of them need tools beyond a flashlight.

  • Find the main water shutoff, usually where the line enters the house, and turn it clockwise until it stops..
  • Stop running sinks, showers, dishwashers and washing machines if a drain is backing up..
  • Keep clear of wet floors near outlets, power strips and the electrical panel..
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 before you call us..
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors and take a few photos for your insurer..

Do not open the city water meter box or touch the meter or setter. Herriman City says tampering can lead to fines, and your own main valve does the same job.

What Herriman plumbing calls have in common

The city describes its own water as very hard. Local wells run roughly 25 to 60 grains per gallon while Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District water runs about 10 to 12. Scale builds up inside water heaters and faucet aerators, so tank flushing and aerator cleaning matter here more than in a soft water town.

Sewer service comes from Jordan Basin Improvement District, not the city. The district maintains the main in the street and the homeowner owns the line from the house to that main. That split decides who pays when roots or a collapsed section block the line, so a camera inspection is the first useful step.

Replacing or adding a water heater needs a city permit, while repair of existing plumbing does not. Gas diagrams are required for tankless units. Knowing that up front keeps a Saturday water heater swap from turning into a failed inspection later.

Quick answers before you call

Is your emergency line answered 24 hours a day?

Yes. The line takes calls at night, on weekends and on holidays. Appointment times depend on the problem and crew availability, and the dispatcher tells you what is realistic for your address.

Do you send licensed plumbers?

Yes. Jobs go to licensed and insured plumbers. Utah licenses plumbers at apprentice, journeyman and master levels, and you can check any license on the state lookup at secure.utah.gov/llv.

How do I know the price before work starts?

The plumber diagnoses the problem first and explains the repair options with the price. Work does not begin until you agree. The cost factors for each service are listed on its page.

What should I do before the plumber arrives?

Shut off the main water valve if water is running, stop using any backing-up drain and stay away from wet areas near electrical equipment. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and Enbridge Gas first.

Do I need a permit for a water heater replacement in Herriman?

The city says replacing or adding a water heater requires a permit, while repair of existing plumbing does not. Ask the building department at 801-446-5327 about current fees and inspection timing.

Which towns do you serve besides Herriman?

Riverton, South Jordan, Bluffdale and West Jordan have their own pages with ZIP codes and local notes. If your town is not listed, call and ask.
