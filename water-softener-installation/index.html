Water Softener and Filtration in Herriman, UT.

Glasses come out of the dishwasher spotted, shower doors turn chalky and the kettle crusts over within weeks. A water softener in Herriman answers that problem at the source, because the city itself calls local water very hard.

The numbers behind Herriman's hard water

The city says local wells usually run between 25 and 60 grains per gallon, while water from the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District runs 10 to 12. On the city's own scale, anything above 10 counts as very hard. A softener sized for 12 grains is working far below capacity if the tap pulls well water at 40.

Because the supply blends, your hardness moves with the season. The city switches to the district's winter source in mid-October and brings wells back as demand rises in spring. That means the water you tested in August may not match what comes out in January.

Practical advice, then, is to test twice, once in winter and once in summer, and set the softener for the harder reading. The city also suggests checking your softener settings with the change of season.

Salt softener, salt-free conditioner or filter

Many homes end up with a softener for the whole house and a small reverse osmosis unit in the kitchen.

A salt-free unit is not a softener, and we will not call it one.

Salt versus salt-free, honestly

An ion exchange softener is the only common device that measurably reduces hardness. Resin beads hold sodium, trade it for calcium and magnesium as water passes, and then flush clean with brine during regeneration.

Salt-free conditioners do not remove minerals. They may cut scale buildup in some cases, but they leave the hardness number where it was and will not give you the slick feel or spot-free dishes. With well water near 40 grains, we usually steer buyers toward a real softener.

Sizing your system

Capacity comes from arithmetic, not guesswork.

  1. Get a hardness reading.

    Use a test kit or ask your water utility. Write down the grains per gallon.

  2. Count daily gallons.

    Estimate about 75 gallons per person per day, which gives 300 for a family of four.

  3. Multiply.

    300 gallons times 12 grains is 3,600 grains per day. At 40 grains it is 12,000 per day.

  4. Choose a regeneration plan.

    A 32,000 grain unit regenerating every week works for the first case, and the second wants a larger tank or more frequent cycles.

  5. Check iron.

    Iron in the water uses up resin capacity, so tell us if you see orange stains.

These figures are general mechanics. Your tap result and your family's habits set the real numbers.

What a proper install includes

Cheap installs often skip the details below.

  • A bypass valve so you can isolate the unit for service..
  • Unions or quick-connects on the inlet and outlet for easy removal..
  • A drain line with an air gap, so wastewater cannot siphon back into the softener or the potable supply..
  • A drain route to a proper standpipe or floor drain, as plumbing code requires..
  • A separate cold hose bib circuit left unsoftened for lawn use where plumbed..
  • Working room around the unit so the resin tank and brine tank can be serviced..

Adding new plumbing lines can require a city permit. Ask the Herriman building department before you plan the drain route.

Backflow and the air gap

A softener drain must never connect directly to the sewer line. An open air gap keeps backflow from carrying brine or wastewater into the equipment. Herriman City reminds residents that hose bibs need vacuum breakers, and the same logic applies to anything connected to potable water.

Secondary water is not your softener's job

Some Herriman areas have secondary irrigation water. The city says it is untreated and not for human consumption, so a softener should only see the culinary supply. If your hose bibs are on secondary, do not tie them into the treated loop.

Water softening also protects tank water heaters. Scale on elements and tank bottoms cuts efficiency, so a softener often ties directly to water heater replacement timing and to tankless units that clog quickly with hardness.

Softener questions before you buy

How hard is Herriman water?

The city says its wells run 25 to 60 grains per gallon and Jordan Valley water 10 to 12, which it calls very hard. Test your tap to see where you land.

Does the water change during the year?

Yes. The city moves to the Jordan Valley winter source in mid-October and brings wells back in spring, so hardness shifts. Check your softener setting each season.

Do I need a permit for a water softener?

Adding new plumbing lines can require a permit, while maintenance of existing plumbing does not. Ask the Herriman building department about your install.

Is a salt-free system as good as a softener?

No, not at removing hardness. It changes scale behavior but does not lower the number, and we will say so.

Will a softener make my water taste salty?

A properly set softener adds only a small amount of sodium. Many people install reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap for drinking.

How often should the softener be serviced?

Refill salt, clean the brine tank and check the settings each season. Resin lasts many years in clean water.

Should the softener feed my outdoor faucets?

Usually not. Lawn water does not need softening, and it wastes salt.
